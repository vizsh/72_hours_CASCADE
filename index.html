One question, asked four ways, every half hour.

What is likely to fail next, what will that failure affect, and what can we do now to stop the cascade? Each layer feeds the next, and every new reading re-runs all four.

  1. What physical event is coming?

    Rain, heat, plumes, crowds and grid faults, carried as 200 possible futures rather than one line.

    Most members put a cloudburst over the city before dawn on 23 September; some miss it entirely.

    Hazard

  2. What does it touch?

    Real rivers, roads, substations, hospitals and 2.9 million modeled residents decide what sits in its path.

    Low ground below Ambazari Lake, the ring-road underpass, dense old neighbourhoods along the Nag.

    Exposure

  3. What fails next, and when?

    Failures propagate through a dependency network the twin learns from the sensor streams. Each link has a mechanism, a lag and a confidence.

    Lake overflow → flooded crossing → ambulance delay → hospital over capacity.

    Cascade

  4. What can we do before the window closes?

    Actions run on the same futures as doing nothing, so you see what breaks, what it costs elsewhere, and the latest time to start.

    Mobile pumps, staged ambulances and an early underpass closure, compared on identical futures.

    Intervention

Inside the operations room

Six instruments that sit on top of the forecast. Each one below is live: it is computed now, in a background thread, from the same engine the operations room uses.

Seven crises, one engine.

Each hazard is a plug-in that says how it stresses city systems. Everything downstream (propagation, discovery, crises, interventions, validation) is shared, and runs unchanged on a city it has never seen.

Honest about what is real.

No Indian city publishes live bed counts, feeder status and ambulance traces. So the forecaster is tested against a hidden world model it never sees, running on real geography.

Real

  • Roads, rivers, lakes, buildings, substations, hospitals, junctions (OpenStreetMap)
  • Live 31-member weather ensembles (Open-Meteo GFS)
  • Storm timing and totals for the 2023 replay (IMD-reported)
  • Population totals by city, distributed by street and building density

Simulated, labelled

  • Drain, feeder, tower, hospital and ambulance telemetry
  • Which link carries how much stress (hidden from the forecaster)
  • Citizen reports, including planted false ones
  • Sensor faults and the red-team attacks you launch yourself
